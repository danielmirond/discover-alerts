If snail mucin is K-Beauty's viral ingredient, centella asiatica is the one with substance. While mucin conquered TikTok, centella has spent decades in the dermatological scientific literature with studies that most cosmetic actives would envy.

*Centella asiatica* (also called gotu kola, tiger grass or pegaga) is a perennial herbaceous plant from the Apiaceae family, native to Southeast Asia. In traditional Chinese medicine, Ayurveda and the European pharmacopoeia it has been used for centuries for wound healing. But what makes it interesting for modern skincare isn't tradition: it's the data.

<EvidenceBadge level={3} label="Strong evidence" />

## Active compounds: the four triterpenoids

Centella is not a simple ingredient. It contains a group of pentacyclic triterpenoids responsible for its biological activity:

### Asiaticoside

The most studied compound. It converts to asiatic acid after skin absorption. Stimulates type I collagen synthesis in fibroblasts. The main compound responsible for wound-healing activity.

### Madecassoside

Potent anti-inflammatory. Inhibits the NF-κB pathway, reducing production of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6). The key compound for reactive and post-procedure skin.

### Asiatic acid

Stimulates collagen production and fibroblast proliferation. Acts directly without needing conversion. Has additional antioxidant activity.

### Madecassic acid

Promotes type III collagen synthesis and skin barrier repair. Contributes to the tissue remodeling phase.

The combination of these four compounds is marketed as **TECA** (Titrated Extract of Centella Asiatica) or **Centella Asiatica Triterpenic Fraction (CATTF)**, and is the form with the most clinical evidence.

## Clinical evidence

### Healing and repair

**[Bylka et al., 2014 (Postepy Dermatologii i Alergologii)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25254008/):** Comprehensive review of centella's dermatological activity. Demonstrated that TECA accelerates wound healing by stimulating type I collagen synthesis (up to +300%) and by increasing tensile strength of newly formed skin. Mechanism: activation of the TGF-β/Smad pathway in fibroblasts.

**[Widgerow et al., 2015 (Aesthetic Plastic Surgery)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26085227/):** Centella reduced hypertrophic scar formation post-surgery compared to placebo. The triterpenoids modulate fibroblast activity to prevent the overproduction of disordered collagen that forms keloids.

### Anti-inflammatory

**[George et al., 2009 (Indian Journal of Pharmaceutical Sciences)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20502539/):** Centella demonstrated anti-inflammatory activity comparable to indomethacin (NSAID) in experimental models. Madecassoside inhibited the NF-κB pathway and reduced COX-2 expression.

### Skin barrier

**[Ratz-Lyko et al., 2016 (Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=centella+asiatica+skin+barrier+repair+ceramides):** Centella extract improved barrier function (measured as transepidermal water loss, TEWL) and increased ceramide production in the stratum corneum.

### Anti-aging

**[Hashim et al., 2011 (Molecules)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=centella+asiatica+collagen+synthesis+anti-aging+wrinkles):** Asiaticoside and asiatic acid stimulated type I collagen production in human fibroblasts in a dose-dependent manner. The effect was comparable to retinol at equivalent concentrations, without the associated irritation.

## "Cica": the K-Beauty term

In K-Beauty, centella is known as **cica** (abbreviation of cicatrization). The explosion of Korean "cica" products began in 2018 and consolidated as one of the largest categories in Asian skincare.

The difference between "centella" and "cica" is marketing, not formulation. Both refer to *Centella asiatica* or its extracts. But "cica" implies a Korean formulation optimized to soothe, repair and protect the skin barrier.

### Why Korea adopted centella

Korean skin tends to be reactive. The combination of high urban pollution (Seoul), extreme temperature changes (dry winter, humid summer) and intensive use of actives (retinol, AHA, vitamin C) creates demand for soothing ingredients. Centella fills that need with real evidence.

## How to use it in your routine

### Position in the routine

1. **Cleansing**
2. **Toner** (you can use cica toner here)
3. **Cica serum/ampoule** ← here as treatment
4. **Actives** (retinol, vitamin C)
5. **Moisturizer** (can be cica cream)
6. **SPF**

### When to use centella

- **After retinol:** centella soothes retinol irritation. Apply cica serum the morning after a retinol night
- **Post-procedure:** after microneedling, chemical peel or laser. Centella accelerates barrier recovery
- **Chronic reactive skin:** as a daily base for skin with rosacea, perioral dermatitis or general sensitivity
- **Post-acne:** on recent marks and scars to accelerate remodeling

### Compatibility

- **Retinol:** excellent synergy (centella soothes, retinol stimulates)
- **Niacinamide:** compatible, ideal combination for oily skin with redness
- **Vitamin C:** compatible, apply vitamin C first
- **AHA/BHA:** compatible, centella soothes post-exfoliation
- **Snail mucin:** compatible, both hydrate and repair
- **Hyaluronic acid:** compatible, HA hydrates + centella repairs

No known incompatibilities. Centella is one of the most versatile and safe actives.

## Centella vs other soothing ingredients

| Ingredient | Mechanism | Evidence | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Centella asiatica** | Triterpenoids: collagen + anti-inflammatory + barrier | Strong (multiple RCTs) | Repair, healing, reactive skin |
| **Allantoin** | Cell proliferation | Moderate | Healing, sensitive skin |
| **Panthenol (B5)** | Hydration + barrier repair | Moderate-strong | Dehydrated skin, post-procedure |
| **Bisabolol** | Anti-inflammatory (inhibits 5-LOX) | Moderate | Eczema, dermatitis |
| **Calendula** | Anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial | Moderate | Irritated skin, babies |
| **Colloidal oatmeal** | Anti-inflammatory, antipruritic | Strong | Eczema, itching, atopic dermatitis |

Centella has the advantage of combining active repair (collagen) with calming (anti-inflammatory). Most soothing ingredients only do one of the two.

## The origin question: Madagascar vs others

Centella asiatica from Madagascar is considered to have the highest concentration of active triterpenoids. The reason: environmental stress conditions (altitude, volcanic soil, intense UV radiation) cause the plant to produce more secondary metabolites as a defense mechanism.

Skin1004 popularized the concept of "Madagascar centella" as a quality indicator. Not all centella extracts are equal: the concentration of asiaticoside and madecassoside varies between 1% and 8% depending on origin and extraction method.

## Oral use: gotu kola

Centella is also taken as an oral supplement (gotu kola) with evidence for:

- **Chronic venous insufficiency:** oral TECA improved symptoms of leg heaviness and edema in a 2001 RCT
- **Anxiety:** preliminary studies show mild anxiolytic effect
- **Cognition:** used in Ayurvedic medicine as a nootropic. modern evidence is limited but promising

Typical oral dose is 500-1000mg of standardized extract per day. Topical and oral use are complementary.
