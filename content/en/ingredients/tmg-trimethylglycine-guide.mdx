TMG (trimethylglycine, also called anhydrous betaine) is a molecule with three methyl groups attached to a glycine. Its main function: donating those methyl groups to essential biochemical reactions. Without enough methyl groups, your body cannot synthesize DNA correctly, regulate gene expression, produce neurotransmitters, or metabolize homocysteine (an amino acid that is toxic to arteries when it accumulates).

In the longevity community, TMG has become the mandatory complement to NMN (nicotinamide mononucleotide). The reason: NMN consumes methyl groups when it is metabolized to NAD+. If you take NMN without TMG, you can deplete your methyl pool and elevate homocysteine. TMG replenishes those methyl groups.

## Mechanism: methylation and homocysteine

### What is methylation

Methylation is the transfer of a methyl group (CH3) to another molecule. It is one of the most frequent biochemical reactions in the body. It occurs billions of times per second and controls:

- **Gene expression:** DNA methylation activates or silences genes (epigenetics)
- **Neurotransmitters:** the synthesis of serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine requires methylation
- **Liver detoxification:** phase II of the liver depends on methylation
- **Homocysteine:** the conversion of homocysteine to methionine requires a methyl donor

### The methionine cycle

1. Methionine is converted to SAM (S-adenosylmethionine), the universal methyl donor
2. SAM donates its methyl group and becomes SAH (S-adenosylhomocysteine)
3. SAH is converted to homocysteine
4. Homocysteine is recycled back to methionine by two pathways:
   - **Folate pathway:** requires vitamin B9 (folate) + B12
   - **Betaine pathway:** TMG donates its methyl group directly to homocysteine

### Why homocysteine matters

Elevated homocysteine (hyperhomocysteinemia) is an independent cardiovascular risk factor. It damages the vascular endothelium, promotes platelet formation, and increases oxidative stress. Levels above 12 umol/L are associated with higher cardiovascular risk.

**[Olthof et al., 2003 (Journal of Nutrition)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12399266/):** Betaine supplementation (6g/day) for 6 weeks reduced fasting plasma homocysteine by 20% in healthy subjects.

**[Schwab et al., 2002 (Archives of Internal Medicine)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14652361/):** Low-dose betaine (1.5g/day) produced an immediate and sustained reduction of plasma homocysteine in healthy men and women.

## TMG and NMN: why they go together

### The problem with NMN without TMG

NMN is converted to NAD+ through the nicotinamide salvage pathway. In this process, excess nicotinamide is methylated by the enzyme NNMT (nicotinamide N-methyltransferase) for elimination. Each methylated nicotinamide molecule consumes a methyl group from SAM.

If you take NMN without replenishing the consumed methyl groups:
1. The SAM pool is depleted
2. Homocysteine accumulates (because there are no methyl groups to recycle it)
3. DNA methylation may be altered

**This does not mean NMN is dangerous.** It means NMN works better (and is safer long-term) if you accompany it with a methyl donor like TMG.

## Documented benefits

### Homocysteine reduction

The most solid evidence for TMG. Multiple RCTs demonstrate 12-20% reductions in plasma homocysteine with doses of 1.5-6g/day.

**[Steenge et al., 2003 (Journal of Nutrition)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15720203/):** Review of betaine intake effects on plasma homocysteine. Doses of 1.5g/day already produce significant reduction. The relationship is dose-dependent up to 6g/day.

### Athletic performance

**[Cholewa et al., 2018 (Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36310589/):** Betaine at 2.5g/day improved body composition (more lean mass, less fat) and muscular power in strength athletes. The mechanism: betaine acts as an osmolyte, protecting cells from osmotic stress during intense exercise.

### Fatty liver

TMG has a documented role in hepatic fat metabolism. Betaine deficiency is associated with hepatic steatosis (fatty liver). Supplementation can support hepatic lipid metabolism.

### Cardiovascular protection

**[Barak et al., 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25662827/):** In addition to reducing homocysteine, betaine demonstrated antithrombotic activity in in vivo models and inhibition of platelet aggregation in vitro.

## TMG vs other methyl donors

| Donor | Mechanism | Advantage | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TMG (betaine)** | Donates methyl via BHMT | Direct, fast, cheap | Does not work in all tissues (only liver and kidney) |
| **SAMe** | Universal methyl donor | Acts in all tissues | Expensive, unstable, can cause anxiety at high doses |
| **Methylfolate (5-MTHF)** | Folate pathway | Works throughout the body | Not everyone metabolizes well (MTHFR) |
| **Methylcobalamin (B12)** | Folate cofactor | Essential for the folate pathway | Needs functional folate to act |

TMG is the most direct and cheapest donor. SAMe is more potent but much more expensive ($30-50/month vs $5/month for TMG). Methylfolate is necessary if you have MTHFR variants.

**Complete methylation stack:**
- TMG 1000mg (direct donor, liver/kidney)
- Methylfolate 400-800mcg (folate pathway donor, whole body)
- Methylcobalamin B12 1000mcg (cofactor)

## Safety

- **Safe doses:** up to 6g/day documented in studies without significant adverse effects
- **Effect on LDL:** at doses of 6g/day, betaine can slightly elevate LDL cholesterol. At 1-3g/day, this effect has not been consistently observed
- **Pregnancy:** betaine is found naturally in foods (beets, spinach, quinoa). Supplementation at moderate doses is considered safe
- **Body odor:** very high doses (>6g) can cause a fishy odor (trimethylamine) in sensitive individuals

## Common mistakes

### 1. Confusing TMG with betaine HCl

Betaine HCl is betaine bonded to hydrochloric acid. It is used as a digestive supplement (increases stomach acidity). It is not the same as TMG. TMG is anhydrous betaine, the methyl donor. Check that the product says "TMG" or "anhydrous betaine" or "trimethylglycine."

### 2. Taking NMN without TMG

If you take NMN for longevity, TMG should be part of the stack. It is not mandatory, but it is the recommended practice by the longevity community to avoid methyl depletion.

### 3. Ignoring homocysteine

If you supplement with TMG, ask your doctor to measure homocysteine in your next blood test. It is a cheap marker that tells you if your methylation is working correctly. Optimal: <10 umol/L.

### 4. Excessive dosing without monitoring LDL

At 6g/day, TMG can raise LDL. If you take high doses, monitor your lipid profile. At 1g/day (the standard dose for NMN complement), this risk is minimal.
